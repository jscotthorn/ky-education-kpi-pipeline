Fayette County Board of Education — Equity Council Committee

Equity Scorecard

School Year 2024–25

Executive Summary

The Fayette County Public Schools Equity Scorecard provides a snapshot of student outcomes across key academic, engagement, and postsecondary indicators. The data shows encouraging progress in several areas, including kindergarten readiness, reading and math proficiency, and postsecondary readiness. District averages have improved across multiple indicators in recent years, and in some areas Fayette County performs at or above the Kentucky state average.

At the same time, the scorecard highlights opportunity gaps between student groups. English Learners and students with disabilities experience the largest gaps across several measures, including math proficiency, graduation rates, and postsecondary readiness. Differences in outcomes across schools also remain significant in some indicators, pointing to the importance of continued focus on equitable access to resources and supports.

The report also reflects the impact of ongoing district initiatives aimed at improving outcomes for all students, including early childhood programs, instructional improvements, multilingual supports, and expanded pathways to college and career readiness. These efforts represent important steps toward addressing disparities and strengthening student success.

The Equity Scorecard is intended to support transparency, guide decision-making, and help the district track progress toward more equitable outcomes. Continued attention to closing opportunity gaps and strengthening targeted supports will be essential to ensuring every student has the opportunity to succeed.

The Council is also making available an online Opportunity Index, which will allow for better, ongoing reporting on these indicators and related school metrics.

Indicator 1 — Early Childhood

Kindergarten Readiness

Data source: Kentucky Department of Education

Indicator Overview

Kindergarten Readiness measures how prepared students are when they first enter the district, reflecting the learning experiences and support they received before public school begins. Because Kentucky’s public preschool programs are income- and need-based, the majority of students entering kindergarten in FCPS have not received pre-K instruction from a Kentucky public school.

Students are assessed using the BRIGANCE screening tool, which measures five areas: academic skills, language, physical development, self-help, and social-emotional development. All students entering kindergarten are normally screened within the first 30 days of school.

Note: Kindergarten readiness is screened before or just as a child enters kindergarten — many FCPS schools screen prior to the start of the school year — so it reflects the learning and support a child received before public school began, not something the receiving school produced. It should not be read as a school performance measure.

Current Status (2024–25)

FCPS District Average48.0%
State Average46.0%Fayette is 2.0 pp above state
Largest GapHispanic or Latino27.0 pp below district average

Key Observations and Recommendations

  • FCPS is 2% higher than the Kentucky State average and the percentages of Kindergarten Readiness have been increasing over the last 3 years.
  • This could be in part due to the FCPS Preschool Program. Preschool is not a mandatory program, but it is available to children who meet the requirements (age, special needs, and/or income). See fcps.net/school-resources/early-childhood-education/preschool for more information.
  • However, there is always an opportunity to improve, especially for our Hispanic or Latino students who have the largest gap of 27% below the district average.
  • The FCPS Classroom on Wheels is a fantastic program that engages preschool resources with the community. See fcps.net/…/classroom-on-wheels for more information. There is an opportunity to increase the resources in locations in Lexington with a high Hispanic or Latino population as well as the number of resources in Spanish.
  • Cardinal Valley Elementary School has the lowest rate of Kindergarten Readiness in FCPS, however their student population is 76% Hispanic or Latino. They are addressing this by utilizing the only heritage-speaker dual language immersion program in the state, and Cardinal Valley Elementary has the highest English Learner Progress Rate in the district, at 68%.

Indicator 2 — Elementary

3rd Grade Reading Proficiency

Data source: Kentucky Department of Education

Indicator Overview

3rd Grade Reading Proficiency is a critical milestone. It marks the transition from learning to read to reading to learn, which is fundamental for success in all other subjects.

Students are assessed on reading comprehension and literacy skills as a component of the Kentucky Summative Assessment (KSA), aligned to Kentucky Academic Standards for Reading and Writing.

Current Status (2024–25)

FCPS District Average50.0%
State Average47.0%Fayette is 3.0 pp above state
Largest GapStudents with Disabilities (IEP)25.0 pp below district average

Key Observations and Recommendations

FCPS 3rd Grade Reading Proficiency is 3 percentage points above the state average, and all student groups have been improving over the last 3 years. African American students have shown the strongest gains of any group (+8 percentage points), reflecting meaningful momentum in closing the achievement gap.

The largest gaps are for Students with Disabilities (IEP) (25 percentage points below the district average) and English Learners (24 percentage points below). While SWD students have gained 3 percentage points over 3 years, EL students have gained just 1 percentage point — nearly flat. Additional early literacy supports targeted at these groups, particularly as science of reading practices are implemented district-wide, represent an important opportunity.

The widest gap that exists is between schools, where we see a 67-percentage-point spread between the highest and lowest-performing elementary schools. The ECC notes that geography and socioeconomic conditions seem highly correlated to school performance for this indicator, and we recommend attention be paid to improving the lowest-performing schools.

Indicator 3 — Middle School

8th Grade Math Proficiency

Data source: Kentucky Department of Education

Indicator Overview

8th Grade Math Proficiency is a key indicator of future academic success.

Students are assessed on mathematics content including linear equations, functions, geometry, and statistics as a component of the Kentucky Summative Assessment (KSA), aligned to Kentucky Academic Standards for Mathematics. Performance contributes to school accountability ratings.

Current Status (2024–25)

FCPS District Average45.0%
State Average40.0%Fayette is 5.0 pp above state
Largest GapEnglish Learner32.0 pp below district average

Key Observations and Recommendations

FCPS 8th Grade Math Proficiency is 5 percentage points above the state average, and every student group is improving. District-wide middle school math proficiency grew from 36% to 43% over the last two years. This reflects the impact of increased academic rigor, alignment with Kentucky Academic Standards, and the addition of instructional coaching at every school. In 2024–25, FCPS also invested $5.8 million in a new districtwide math curriculum. See fcps.net/academic-programs for more information.

Despite broad improvement, English Learners (13%, 32 percentage points below the district average) and Students with Disabilities (IEP) (14%, 31 percentage points below) face the largest gaps. Encouragingly, both groups are among the fastest improving — EL students have gained 8 percentage points over 3 years, tied for the strongest trend of any group. Sustaining and accelerating these gains will require continued targeted support, particularly given how much ground remains to close.

SCAPA at Bluegrass (90%) is the highest-performing school for 8th grade math by a significant margin. The 68-percentage-point range between SCAPA and Lexington Traditional Magnet (22%) raises an important equity question: how can the high expectations and accelerated model at schools like SCAPA be more broadly accessible to students across the district? See fcps.net/…/accelerated-learning-opportunities for more information on FCPS accelerated learning programs.

Indicator 4 — High School

9th Grade On-Track

Data source: Fayette County Public Schools

Indicator Overview

9th Grade On-Track measures the percentage of 9th graders who meet promotion criteria by the end of the school year, meaning they have earned enough credits to be on track toward graduation.

Students who fall off-track in 9th grade are significantly less likely to graduate on time. This indicator is tracked for Fayette County high schools only.

Note: State-level comparison data is not available for this indicator.

Current Status (2024–25)

FCPS District Average84.0%
State AverageN/A
Largest GapEnglish Learner17.0 pp below district average

Key Observations and Recommendations

FCPS defines a student as “9th grade on-track” if they earn a minimum of 5.5 credits AND received a Math credit AND received an English credit, making them “on track” to graduate in four years. This is a locally-developed measure — Kentucky does not currently have a formal statewide definition for 9th grade on-track status or include it in the state report card, making FCPS one of the relatively few districts in the state systematically tracking this leading indicator of graduation. In 2024–25, 84% of FCPS 9th graders met this benchmark. FCPS has invested in multiple programs to support successful high school transitions, including Freshman Academies at Bryan Station, Tates Creek, and Frederick Douglass — small learning communities that provide dedicated counselors, team-based teaching, and a required Freshman Seminar covering study skills, goal setting, and career exploration — as well as the Summer Ignite Transition Week, which orients incoming 9th graders to their new school before the year begins. See fcps.net/academic-programs/academies-of-lexington for more information.

English Learners face the largest on-track gap of any group: just 67% of EL students met the on-track threshold in 2024–25, 17 percentage points below the district average, and this gap has remained essentially flat over the past three years (−0.8 pp). Students with Disabilities (IEP) and Hispanic or Latino students also trail the district average (78.5% and 75.4% respectively), though both groups are improving — Students with Disabilities gained 4.9 percentage points over three years, the strongest trend of any demographic group. For students who do fall off-track, FCPS offers credit recovery through eSchool (an online platform available year-round) and the Re-engagement Center at Success Academy, which serves students ages 16+ who are at least one year behind in credits and need a flexible, accelerated pathway to graduation. See success.fcps.net/re-engagement-center for more information.

Indicator 9 — Cross-Grade

School Climate Index

Data source: Kentucky Department of Education

Indicator Overview

School Climate Index measures students’ sense of belonging, safety, and engagement at school. A positive school climate is essential for student well-being and academic success.

This indicator captures student perceptions of whether their school is caring and welcoming, whether rules are fair, whether adults care about and support students, and whether students feel encouraged and part of their school. It is measured through 14 survey questions on a scale from Strongly Disagree to Strongly Agree.

Current Status (2024–25)

FCPS District Average78.7 pts
State Average80.0 ptsFayette is 1.3 pts below state
Largest GapEnglish Learner1.9 pts below district average

Key Observations and Recommendations

The School Climate Index reflects student perceptions of belonging, fairness, adult support, and engagement. In 2024–25, FCPS scored 78.7 points — 1.3 points below the state average of 80.0, making this one of the few indicators in this report where FCPS trails the state. FCPS has made substantial investments in programs designed to improve school climate, including Positive Behavioral Interventions and Supports (PBIS), a district-wide framework with universal behavioral expectations and tiered intervention; Social Emotional Learning (SEL) programming with twice-yearly DESSA assessments at all grade levels; and a dedicated department for Unity, Belonging, and Student Efficacy focused on creating psychologically safe environments for historically marginalized students. The district also maintains a comprehensive 10-Point Safety Investment Plan covering school culture, mental health, prevention, and physical safety. See fcps.net/…/social-emotional-learning and fcps.net/…/positive-behavior-intervention-and-supports for more information.

Scores across demographic groups have been largely stable over the past three years, with modest declines for most groups. African American students and Students with Disabilities (IEP) have seen the largest dips (3.0 points each over three years), followed by English Learners (−2.3 points) and Hispanic or Latino students (−2.1 points). These are movements worth monitoring but not yet a cause for alarm. The more notable observation is the persistent gap between these groups and the district average, which reflects an ongoing equity dimension in how students experience school climate.

The school-level breakdown tells an important structural story. Elementary schools score in the low-to-mid 70s–80s, with meaningful variation between campuses (11-point range) that likely reflects school-specific differences in culture and practice. Middle schools score lower overall and show the widest variation (13 points), suggesting climate is less consistent at that level. All six high schools are clustered tightly between 65 and 67 points, suggesting the challenge at the high school level is not campus-specific.

Indicator 10 — Cross-Grade

English Learner Proficiency

Data source: Kentucky Department of Education

Indicator Overview

English Learner Proficiency measures the percentage of English learners making meaningful progress in English language acquisition, as assessed by the WIDA ACCESS for ELLs assessment.

Students are counted as making progress if they received a nonzero score on the ACCESS value table — specifically those scoring at the 60–80, 100, or 140 point levels. A score of 0 indicates no measurable progress. This combined rate captures all students demonstrating any level of English language growth, reported separately for elementary, middle, and high school.

Key Observations and Recommendations

FCPS English Learner progress rates have been improving over the last 3 years, with the strongest performance at the elementary level (72%), where Fayette outperforms the state average by 2 percentage points. Most student groups at the elementary level have seen significant gains, with White (non-Hispanic) students up 21 percentage points and African American students up 16 percentage points over 3 years. FCPS’s investment in Multilingual Services — including 8 Multilingual Family Ambassadors serving students across 87 languages and 180+ countries, a Multilingual Center connecting families to food, housing, and employment resources, and the FCPS Lau Plan — supports the broader EL community. The number of FCPS schools flagged for low EL achievement under federal accountability dropped from 17 in 2022 to just 6 in 2024. See fcps.net/families/multilingual-services for more information.

There is a significant opportunity at the secondary level. Progress rates drop sharply from elementary (72%) to middle school (40%) and high school (38%), and Fayette falls 3 percentage points below the state average at the high school level. At the high school level, African American students have seen a decline of 3 percentage points over the last 3 years, while White (non-Hispanic) students are 15 percentage points above the district average. Strengthening secondary-level language supports and examining what is driving the decline among African American high school EL students should be a priority.

Students with Disabilities (IEP) have the largest gap at all three school levels — 13 percentage points below the district average at elementary, 7 at middle school, and 3 at high school — and show little improvement at any level. This suggests an opportunity for deeper coordination between EL and special education services.

Maxwell Spanish Immersion Elementary School (95%) and Cardinal Valley Elementary’s heritage-speaker dual language immersion program are notable bright spots demonstrating the impact of intentional language programming. See fcps.net/academic-programs/world-language-programs for more information on FCPS dual language and world language programs.
